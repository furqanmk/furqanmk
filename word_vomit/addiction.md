# The Social Delimma 

Welcome to all the search engine scrapers, LLM bots and the one friend who chose to open this link - here I share my journey of removing an addiction from my life. I hope you benefit in your life or in retraining your model. Here I go:

## Struggles

I have always been bad at getting things done, seeing things through to completion. I have, what might be very familiar to someone with ADHD, rapid rise in brain hyperactivity and then sudden fall in attention which usually comes in my way to achieve things that I dream of. Although I dream a lot. I dream big all the time but I am bad at execution. 

Every few nights I get super excited about waking up early, like 5:30am in the morning, I download the best rated alarm app, write myself notes and post them in the bathroom, program my thermostat to heat up the room to make it harder for me to go back to bed but I have never had success. Or at least consistent success.

But when I did a few screens for ADHD, the rating did not suggest that I should go through a formal diagnosis. So it seems, the thing I have is not what the medical world calls ADHD but something induced by the excessive use of technology, specially addictive technology that is no good in the longer run - all things considered: social media.

I at one point got addicted to watching car reviews, I was taking a career break at that time and did not have much to do. I was spending over 4 hours each day doing nothing but watching lengthy car reviews. Did I learn something useful, maybe. But was it useful enough to have cost me 4 hours a day for about a month?

At one point my body was sick of watching YouTube for such long periods but my brain was addicted. I have had similar patches with Twitter and Instagram. I suppose most of the Millennials and Gen Zers suffer from this addiction.

## The Journey

Of all the things I have failed at in my life, one thing that I have succeeded in doing is getting rid of social media, at least from my phone. 


### Instagram

I started by deleting Instagram. It took a few tries. Every now and then I would take a photo that I felt proud of and my brain would play a trick on me, saying it's worth it to download the app and share this masterpiece with friends. It's funny how the author on social media thinks of what they create or write as something people will spend a long time appreciating. But in reality, that random person on your follower list who likes every second thing on their feed spent close to 5 seconds deciding between your "masterpiece" is worth a double-tap or a swipe-up. But that is how big social media companies want everyone to think so everyone stays stuck in the circle of quick dopamine shots. 

The one good thing about my condition is that my determination sporadically renews itself but the renewals are short-lived. However, if I manage to act inside the small window of determination - I get stuff done. That's how I have survived and gained whatever success I have. So the seed was planted in a corner of my brain that I needed to somehow get rid of social media from my life but I hadn't yet figured out how the whole journey would feel like. So I kept acting on my episodes and kept deleting Instagram enough times that it hasn't been on my phone for nearly all of the past 2 years.

Thankfully I never acquired the taste for anything like Snapchat or TikTok so the task was made slightly easier. But still not a walk in the park.

### Twitter

Next, Twitter (I won't be calling it what Elon Musk wants us to call it). This was not very straightforward - the addicted part of the brain had good reasons to give me whenever I felt like I was ready to attack this addiction and get it off my phone. The reasons were "it's your source of unbiased news", "it's how you stay connected with current affairs", "you can follow what the big players of industry XYZ are saying" and on and on. But then the world went crazy and there were slaughters in Gaza which I could no longer stomach watching. I know it sounds cowardly but it was getting on my mental health and I had no option but to stop looking at it. I deleted Twitter.

### Facebook

Facebook - well this one's interesting as well. I had deleted my original Facebook account years ago and then created a new one just for Marketplace. For a very long time I did not accept any friends request until an old school friend sent me a WhatsApp message protesting why I had a Facebook but not her on the friends list. And now I have a handful of "friends" on the thing but I don't look at what they are posting. Facebook is nearly dead itself so it was easy to kill it fully.

### YouTube

The process went on for a couple years but in 2025 I have finally deleted the last social media app from my phone: YouTube. It probably was the most difficult because my brain kept convincing me that I only learn from YouTube, and it's not really social media, etc. But when Google introduced the shorts - it was easier for me to convince my addicted brain that YouTube was part of the problem.

## 👋

I have a few things I have done that I am proud of - many more that I wish I did do or still do sometime in the future. But having completed this long process of cleansing my life of social media is something I wanna keep a medal for to myself. This monologue is the medal.
